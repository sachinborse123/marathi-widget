Explicit halant: add ~ right after a consonant to end a word on it with no vowel sound — swapn~ → स्वप्न्. Same marker used for the rare eyelash-र: kuR~haaD → कुऱ्हाड. The apostrophe ' is now always literal punctuation (useful for quoting a word, e.g. 'वाऱ्याने') — it no longer triggers halant.
Why this matters: in this scheme capitals aren't just "alt spellings" — t and T, or s and Sh, are genuinely different Marathi letters (dental त vs. retroflex ट, स vs. ष). Two consonants typed back-to-back with no vowel between them automatically form a जोडाक्षर — namaskaar → नमस्कार. To keep two consonants separate (each keeping its own अ), type the vowel explicitly between them, e.g. bolato not bolto.
Watch for auto-capitalize: since capitals mean a different letter here, a keyboard that auto-capitalizes the first letter of a word can silently change what you meant — e.g. nishchit (निश्चित) becoming Nishchit (णिश्चित) if n gets capitalized to N. This input box has auto-capitalize/auto-correct switched off to prevent that; if you're pasting from elsewhere, double-check the first letter of each word.
